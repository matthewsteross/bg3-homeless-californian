## How vanilla underwear works (everything we learned)
- An item's **root template** (`RootTemplates`) has an `Equipment` node: `Slot` = `Underwear`, a `ParentRace` list, and `Visuals`, a map from a body-type key GUID to a **VisualBank resource id**. The vanilla map has 20 keys (every race x body shape x sex). Our clones copy that map, so they fit every body.
- `Equipment/VisualSet/MaterialOverrides` carries the **colour tints** (`Color_01..03`, `Cloth_Primary`, `Leather_*`, `Metal_*`, vec3). That is all the ten sets change.
- The VisualBank resource (e.g. `HUM_F_CLT_Humans_Underwear_C`, id `69d9e1e0-7548-00a4-15ef-a52622f9ceb1`, in Shared.pak `Public/Shared/Content/Assets/Characters/Humans/[PAK]_Female_Clothing/_merged.lsf`) names the GR2 mesh (`Generated/Public/Shared/Assets/Characters/_Models/Humans/_Female/Resources/HUM_F_CLT_Humans_Underwear_C.GR2`, in Models.pak), `Slot` = `Underwear`, `MaterialType 13`, and one MaterialID per LOD. It also has **`VertexColorMaskSlots`** (`Private_Parts`, `ModestyLeaf`, `Nipple Covers`): these say which parts of the nude body are hidden or covered under the garment. A custom revealing garment must choose these slots deliberately.
- Its material uses the shader `Public/Shared/Assets/Materials/Characters/CHAR_BASE_MSK_VertCut_VT.lsf`: a **tintable mask shader with a virtual texture** (`MSKcloth` mask plus `virtualtexture`). That is why colours are easy and patterns are not: the pattern lives in a virtual texture we cannot reuse or author directly.
- The vanilla meshes are skinned (Underwear_C: 2 LODs, 9,081 vertices, 16,678 triangles, 84 skeleton nodes). Divine converts a GR2 to DAE with skin controllers: `Divine -a convert-model -s <gr2> -d <abs dae> -i gr2 -o dae`.

## What it takes to make the real thing (in order of effort)
1. **Our own lace look (texture):** write our own VisualBank resource + MaterialBank resource + TextureBank (like `hc_*.py` prop `_visual_resource`) that still points at the vanilla GR2 mesh but uses a **non-VT character shader** with our own base-colour / normal / physical maps. Find a suitable shader in Materials.pak (list: `Divine -a list-package -s .../Data/Materials.pak`; there are `CHAR_BASE_*` variants, including `AlphaTested` ones). Lace and see-through areas = alpha-tested texture (mask the holes in the alpha channel). Then point the root template's `VisualSet/MaterialOverrides/MaterialResource` or the Visuals map at our new resource. Unknowns: which shader accepts BaseColor/NormalMap/PhysicalMap plus alpha cut-out on skinned meshes, and whether `MaterialType 13` must be kept.
2. **Different shape (silhouette):** edit vertex positions in the DAE that Divine writes (keep the skin weights, the controller and the skeleton), then `Divine -a convert-model ... -e conform --conform-path <vanilla GR2>` back to GR2 like the props pipeline does. Blender 5.2 has no DAE export, so edit the DAE text/arrays in Python (numpy). Remove or add triangles while keeping the per-vertex weight arrays consistent.
3. **Brand new geometry:** build new triangles on the nude body surface (extract `HUM_F_NKD_Body*.GR2`, offset a patch of faces outwards, add straps and trims), transfer the skin weights from the body vertices, write skin + weights into a DAE, conform to the vanilla skeleton. Do human female first (`HUM_F`), then `HUM_FS` (strong), then other races.
4. Per-body-type work: each body type is its own VisualBank resource and mesh. Plan human female and human female strong first, then add others through the same Visuals map pattern.
